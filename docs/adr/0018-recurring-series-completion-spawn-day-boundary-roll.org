#+title: ADR 0018: Recurring Series — Materialise-on-Completion with a Day-Boundary Roll-Forward
- *Date:* 2026-10-28
- *Status:* Accepted (the engine lands with chunk DR-6/2; storage & sync DR-6/3, surfaces DR-6/4, habits DR-6/5–6/6 follow)
- *Depends on:* ADR 0006 (the interpret loop), ADR 0004 (LWW / sync policy), ADR 0013 (the =(reminder, occurrence)= key precedent)

* Context

Issue #212 asks for recurring tasks. The design round (2026-10-26,
D-C1–D-C13) fixed the shape: *the open head is the template*. A
recurring task is an ordinary card carrying a =RecurRule= (series id,
anchor date, interval pattern, bound); completing the head produces the
next occurrence as a *real card*, and a head left open across a day
boundary rolls forward to the current day. Two alternatives were
rejected:

- *Display-only roll* — keep the stored date stale and add "+overdue"
  at read time. Two truths for one field: every consumer (reminders,
  today view, sync wire, CLI) would need the same virtual arithmetic,
  and the sync wire would publish a lie.
- *Virtual-instance projection* — store one template and project
  occurrences per date range. The sync layer cannot publish a
  projection (the remote sees a deck of real cards), and history
  ("which occurrences did I actually do?") would need a second
  bookkeeping structure the materialized cards give for free.

* Decision

1. *Completion spawns the successor inside the same pure plan.*
   =SetTaskDone= on a recur-bearing head either appends the successor
   card's batch (fresh id, copied title/description/stack/content
   decorations, open, newborn clocks, live labels re-stamped,
   =recur= copied verbatim — same series identity) or, when the
   series' bound is exhausted, performs the *terminal transition*
   (decision 3). A plain task's completion plan is byte-identical to
   the pre-series catalogue. The planner stays pure and total: no
   async, no tick, no IO — the spawn is more plan output.
2. *Midpoint insertion after the head.* The successor's =order= is a
   free slot at =head.order + 1= when the head is last, the floor
   midpoint when the gap to the next card is ≥ 2, and a tie with *the
   head only* when the gap is exactly 1 (resolved by the canonical
   =(order, id)= sort over UUIDv7 minting; the tie vanishes when the
   head leaves the open lists). D-C13's later habit drags reuse the
   same helper.
3. *=Until= is arithmetic; =Count= is history.* An =Until(d)= bound
   filters at the schedule (a member *on* =d= still spawns/rolls).
   =Count(n)= derives from completion history at evaluation time —
   every row carrying the series and a done stamp counts, including
   soft-deleted ones (history outlives deletion; no counter column
   exists). *Suppression happens at the completion*: the exhausted
   completion still stamps done (closes and dismissals intact) and the
   same batch clears =recur= as an appdata-lane edit. Roll-path
   suppression writes nothing — a bound-exhausted open head keeps its
   rule and stays stale until completed or deleted.
4. *The day tick beside the reminder tick.* The state engine's timer
   arm wakes for either engine; each runs under its own flag. The day
   tick reads the injected clock once, plans the roll purely
   (=plan_day_rolls=), persists, then publishes — the reminder tick's
   shape. The watermark (last rolled window-date) is engine-internal
   edge state, *never persisted as user state*: a quiet day is a full
   no-op, a failed batch retries on the next tick without advancing
   the watermark, and a restart re-scans once — which is the boot
   catch-up path and is idempotent by the stale predicate's
   construction. There is deliberately no user-facing switch: the roll
   is the feature's core materialization, and a kiosk with reminders
   off still needs its series to appear.
5. *Date conventions.* =DayCalendar= (day-start time + fixed boot
   offset) is injected everywhere. Field *references* (the head's
   series day) use plain offset-calendar dates — the spawn is
   drift-free from generation to generation; *"today"* is the
   window-date (the night-owl rule). Time-of-day is preserved per
   field. The reference chain is scheduled → duedate → completion day;
   a dateless head is never stale and spawns dateless.
6. *Occurrences roam as cards.* The successor syncs through the
   ordinary =CreateTask= / =AssignLabel= / =UpdateTask= ops; =recur=
   rides the per-task appdata entry behind one =UpdateAppData= flush
   trigger. The moved due date of a roll is a new
   =(reminder, occurrence)= key, so due-anchored reminders re-arm by
   construction — no reminder machinery changes. Two devices rolling
   the same head on the same local day produce identical targets (the
   pure arithmetic), so the no-dedup race is benign under LWW.
   Fold-time dedup for the spawn race is chunk DR-6/3's and keys
   =(series, scheduled-date)=.

* Consequences

- *Born-stale successors converge late.* A multi-day-stale head
  completed mid-day spawns a successor dated in the past; it rolls at
  the *next* boundary, not instantly. Schedule-truth over display
  comfort; the overdue-recurring presentation is DR-6/4's.
- *The terminal clear drops the final occurrence's series
  attribution.* After the bound-exhausted completion the done head no
  longer carries =recur.series=, so derived analysis (streaks,
  occurrence outcomes) cannot see that final completion. Ruled
  deliberately ("the series ends as an ordinary standalone task");
  the analysis surfaces must read the ruling the same way.
- *Daily-series card volume is accepted* (D-C6): a daily chore is one
  card per day, forever, like any deck task.
- *The timer coupling:* an engine with reminders disabled but the roll
  armed wakes on =[reminders] tick_secs= — one clock read + date
  compare per wake, bounded by the watermark to one scan per local
  day.
- *Offset fixed at boot:* a DST flip mid-run shifts wall-clock labels
  ≤1h — already accepted by the day-window ruling; the roll inherits
  it.
